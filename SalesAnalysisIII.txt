/*

1084. Sales Analysis III

Table: Product

+--------------+---------+
| Column Name  | Type    |
+--------------+---------+
| product_id   | int     |
| product_name | varchar |
| unit_price   | int     |
+--------------+---------+
product_id is the primary key (column with unique values) of this table.
Each row of this table indicates the name and the price of each product.
Table: Sales

+-------------+---------+
| Column Name | Type    |
+-------------+---------+
| seller_id   | int     |
| product_id  | int     |
| buyer_id    | int     |
| sale_date   | date    |
| quantity    | int     |
| price       | int     |
+-------------+---------+
This table can have duplicate rows.
product_id is a foreign key (reference column) to the Product table.
Each row of this table contains some information about one sale.
 

Write a solution to report the products that were only sold in the first quarter of 2019. That is, between 2019-01-01 and 2019-03-31 inclusive.

Return the result table in any order.

The result format is in the following example.

 

Example 1:

Input: 
Product table:
+------------+--------------+------------+
| product_id | product_name | unit_price |
+------------+--------------+------------+
| 1          | S8           | 1000       |
| 2          | G4           | 800        |
| 3          | iPhone       | 1400       |
+------------+--------------+------------+
Sales table:
+-----------+------------+----------+------------+----------+-------+
| seller_id | product_id | buyer_id | sale_date  | quantity | price |
+-----------+------------+----------+------------+----------+-------+
| 1         | 1          | 1        | 2019-01-21 | 2        | 2000  |
| 1         | 2          | 2        | 2019-02-17 | 1        | 800   |
| 2         | 2          | 3        | 2019-06-02 | 1        | 800   |
| 3         | 3          | 4        | 2019-05-13 | 2        | 2800  |
+-----------+------------+----------+------------+----------+-------+
Output: 
+-------------+--------------+
| product_id  | product_name |
+-------------+--------------+
| 1           | S8           |
+-------------+--------------+
Explanation: 
The product with id 1 was only sold in the spring of 2019.
The product with id 2 was sold in the spring of 2019 but was also sold after the spring of 2019.
The product with id 3 was sold after spring 2019.
We return only product 1 as it is the product that was only sold in the spring of 2019.






*/



================================================================================================================================




select P.product_id,P.product_name from Product P INNER JOIN Sales S on P.product_id = S.product_id
GROUP BY P.product_id, P.product_name
HAVING MIN(S.sale_date) >= DATE '2019-01-01'
   AND MAX(S.sale_date) <= DATE '2019-03-31';


================================================================================================================================


the problem asks for products that were sold exclusively during the first quarter of 2019 — meaning 
the product must not have been sold before January 1 or after March 31.


3. Understand the approach
We use GROUP BY, MIN(), MAX(), and HAVING.

Suppose the Sales table contains:

product_id	sale_date
1			2019-01-10
1			2019-02-15
1			2019-04-05
2			2019-01-20
2			2019-03-20
3			2018-12-20
3			2019-02-10


- Product 1: Excluded because it was sold in April.
- Product 2: Included because all sales occurred during January–March.
- Product 3: Excluded because it was sold in December 2018.


Why do we use MIN() and MAX()?
- MIN(S.sale_date) finds the earliest sale date for each product.
- MAX(S.sale_date) finds the latest sale date for each product.
The HAVING condition ensures both dates fall within the required period. Therefore, all sales for that product occurred within the period.


Remember this for SQL interviews:
- WHERE filters individual rows before grouping.
- HAVING filters groups after GROUP BY.


================================================================================================================================

1. First, understand the requirement
We need to find products that were sold only between January 1, 2019, and March 31, 2019.
This means:
- A product sold in January, February, and March → Include it.
- A product sold in February and April → Exclude it.
- A product sold in December 2018 and February 2019 → Exclude it.
The important point is that every sale of a product must fall within the given date range.


2. Let's understand with sample data
Suppose we have this Sales table:
product_id	sale_date
1	2019-01-10
1	2019-02-15
1	2019-03-20
2	2019-02-10
2	2019-04-05
3	2018-12-20
3	2019-02-15
We want to check each product's sales dates.


3. Why GROUP BY?
GROUP BY combines rows belonging to the same product so that we can examine all its sales together.
For example:

GROUP BY product_id

Conceptually, the data becomes:
Product 1
Jan 10 → Feb 15 → Mar 20
All sales are within the required period.


Product 2
Feb 10 → Apr 5
One sale is outside the period.


Product 3
Dec 20 → Feb 15
One sale is before the period.


Without GROUP BY, SQL processes individual rows rather than calculating the earliest and latest dates for each product.


4. Why MIN()?
MIN(sale_date)


MIN() finds the earliest sale date for each product.
product_id	Earliest sale (MIN)
1	2019-01-10
2	2019-02-10
3	2018-12-20
We need to make sure the earliest sale is not before January 1, 2019.
MIN(sale_date) >= DATE '2019-01-01'


Product 3 fails this condition because its earliest sale was in December 2018.


5. Why MAX()?
MAX(sale_date)


MAX() finds the latest sale date for each product.
product_id	Latest sale (MAX)
1	2019-03-20
2	2019-04-05
3	2019-02-15
We need to make sure the latest sale is not after March 31, 2019.
MAX(sale_date) <= DATE '2019-03-31'


Product 2 fails because its latest sale was in April.
Together, MIN() and MAX() check both boundaries of the date range. If the earliest and latest sales are within the range, all sales dates between them must also be within that range.


6. Why HAVING instead of WHERE?
This is the key concept.
- WHERE filters individual rows before grouping.
- HAVING filters groups after grouping and aggregation.
We need to filter products based on their calculated earliest and latest sale dates. That's why we use HAVING.
