🔑 What is DENSE_RANK()?
Purpose: Assigns a ranking number to rows based on an ORDER BY clause, but without skipping numbers when ties occur.

Syntax:
DENSE_RANK() OVER ( [PARTITION BY column] ORDER BY column ASC|DESC )

PARTITION BY (optional): Groups rows (e.g., by department).

ORDER BY (required): Defines ranking order (e.g., by salary).

📊 Example with Employees Table
================================
SELECT first_name, last_name, salary,
       DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY salary DESC) AS salary_rank
FROM employees;


Output (simplified):
====================

Employee	Salary	Rank
Alice	     9000	1
Bob	         9000	1
Charlie	     8000	2
Diana	     7000	3

 Charlie gets rank 2 (not 3), because DENSE_RANK() keeps ranks consecutive even after ties.

⚖️ Difference from RANK()
===========================
RANK() → Skips numbers after ties (Alice & Bob tie at 1, Charlie becomes 3).

DENSE_RANK() → No gaps; ranks remain consecutive (Alice & Bob tie at 1, Charlie becomes 2).

⚠️ Beginner Tips
Use DENSE_RANK() when you want continuous ranking without gaps.

Use RANK() when you want to reflect real-world competition (like Olympic medals).

Always pair with ORDER BY inside OVER() — otherwise Oracle won’t know how to rank rows.

📌 Query-Based Interview Questions & Answers
***********************************************
Q1: Find the top 3 highest-paid employees
Query:
SELECT first_name, last_name, salary
FROM (
  SELECT first_name, last_name, salary,
         RANK() OVER (ORDER BY salary DESC) AS rnk
  FROM employees
)
WHERE rnk <= 3;

Answer:  
Ranks employees by salary and filters only those with rank ≤ 3. Handles ties correctly.



Q2: Find the second-highest salary in the company
SELECT salary
FROM (
  SELECT salary,
         DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY salary DESC) AS rnk
  FROM employees
)
WHERE rnk = 2;

Answer:  
Uses DENSE_RANK() to avoid gaps and fetches the second-highest salary even if multiple employees share it.


Q3: Rank employees within each department by salary
Query:

SELECT department_id, first_name, salary,
       RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY department_id ORDER BY salary DESC) AS dept_rank
FROM employees;

Answer:  
Partitions data by department, ranking employees separately within each group.


Q4: Show employees who are top earners in their department
Query:
SELECT department_id, first_name, salary
FROM (
  SELECT department_id, first_name, salary,
         RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY department_id ORDER BY salary DESC) AS dept_rank
  FROM employees
)
WHERE dept_rank = 1;

Answer:  
Filters only employees with rank 1 in their department — useful for identifying departmental leaders.


Q5: Compare RANK(), DENSE_RANK(), and ROW_NUMBER() outputs
Query:
SELECT first_name, salary,
       RANK() OVER (ORDER BY salary DESC) AS rnk,
       DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY salary DESC) AS dense_rnk,
       ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY salary DESC) AS row_num
FROM employees;

Answer:

RANK() → skips numbers after ties.

DENSE_RANK() → no gaps, consecutive ranks.

ROW_NUMBER() → unique sequence, no ties.
